Lecture 07 · Module 2 · Robotics Dynamics

2-Link Planar Arm: From Kinematics to Torque Equations

A no-skips walkthrough — transformation matrices, energies, Lagrangian, and Euler–Lagrange torques.

Lecturer. bibliosense Lecture. 07 Course. Robotics Dynamics
Today we slow down and do the whole derivation for a 2-link planar arm with tip masses — every square expanded, every chain rule shown, every $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$ called out by name. We start from the homogeneous transformation matrices, walk through positions and velocities, build the kinetic and potential energies, write the Lagrangian, and finally crank the Euler–Lagrange machine to get explicit torque equations $\tau_1$ and $\tau_2$. By the end you should have a template you can re-use for any open-chain manipulator.

0What this lecture covers

We work through a single sustained example: a planar 2-link arm with point masses $m_1$ and $m_2$ at the distal ends of links of length $L_1$ and $L_2$, with joint angles $\theta_1$ and $\theta_2$. The goal is to produce the closed-form torque equations $\tau_1, \tau_2$ that govern its dynamics.

The path we follow is the standard Lagrangian recipe: transformations → positions → velocities → kinetic energy $T$ → potential energy $V$ → Lagrangian $L = T - V$ → Euler–Lagrange equations. Nothing is hand-waved — every cross term and every chain-rule step is written out.

If you can do this one cleanly, you can do any serial manipulator. The bookkeeping is the hard part, not the physics.
Setup. Planar arm in the vertical plane, gravity $g$ acting in $-y$. Tip masses only — link mass and inertia are ignored to keep the algebra readable.
Why this matters. These explicit equations feed straight into Module 3, where we simulate and control the arm using the Euler–Lagrange dynamics derived here.

1Homogeneous transformations for each link

We attach a frame to each link and write the homogeneous transformation from the base. For link 1:

$T_1 = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_1 & -\sin\theta_1 & L_1\cos\theta_1 \\ \sin\theta_1 & \cos\theta_1 & L_1\sin\theta_1 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

For link 2 expressed in the base frame (so we get $m_2$'s position directly):

$T_2 = \begin{bmatrix} \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) & -\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) & L_1\cos\theta_1 + L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) \\ \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) & \cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) & L_1\sin\theta_1 + L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$

The last column of each matrix is what we actually use — it carries the tip position of the link.

Convention. Angles are measured from the previous link's axis. $\theta_2$ is the relative angle at joint 2, which is why link 2's orientation in the base frame is $\theta_1+\theta_2$.

2Tip-mass positions

Reading the last column of each $T_i$:

For $m_1$ (end of link 1):
$x_1 = L_1\cos\theta_1, \qquad y_1 = L_1\sin\theta_1$

For $m_2$ (end of link 2):
$x_2 = L_1\cos\theta_1 + L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$
$y_2 = L_1\sin\theta_1 + L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$

These are pure geometry — no time derivatives yet.

3Velocities by the chain rule

Differentiate the positions with respect to time, remembering that only the joint angles $\theta_1, \theta_2$ depend on $t$.

For $m_1$:
$\dot{x}_1 = -L_1\sin\theta_1\,\dot{\theta}_1, \qquad \dot{y}_1 = L_1\cos\theta_1\,\dot{\theta}_1$

For $m_2$:
$\dot{x}_2 = -L_1\sin\theta_1\,\dot{\theta}_1 - L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\,(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)$
$\dot{y}_2 = L_1\cos\theta_1\,\dot{\theta}_1 + L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)\,(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)$

The sum $(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)$ comes straight from $\frac{d}{dt}\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$.

If you ever lose track of a sign, redo this step from scratch — it's where the bugs hide.

4Squared speeds $v_1^2$ and $v_2^2$

Square and add — but expand everything so we can see the structure.

For $m_1$:
$v_1^2 = \dot{x}_1^2 + \dot{y}_1^2 = L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2(\sin^2\theta_1 + \cos^2\theta_1) = L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2$

For $m_2$, expanding both squares and grouping:

$\begin{aligned} v_2^2 =\;& L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2\,(\sin^2\theta_1 + \cos^2\theta_1) \\ &+ L_2^2(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)^2\,(\sin^2(\theta_1+\theta_2)+\cos^2(\theta_1+\theta_2)) \\ &+ 2L_1L_2\,\dot{\theta}_1(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)\,[\sin\theta_1\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + \cos\theta_1\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)] \end{aligned}$

Using $\sin^2+\cos^2=1$ and the cosine-difference identity $\cos\alpha\cos\beta + \sin\alpha\sin\beta = \cos(\alpha-\beta)$, this collapses to:

$\boxed{\;v_2^2 = L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2 + L_2^2(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)\;}$

Identity used. $\cos\theta_1\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) + \sin\theta_1\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) = \cos(\theta_1 - (\theta_1+\theta_2)) = \cos(-\theta_2) = \cos\theta_2$. That's where the $\cos\theta_2$ coupling term comes from.

5Total kinetic energy $T$

Add the contributions of both point masses:

$T = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1 v_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2 v_2^2$

Substituting our expressions:

$T = \tfrac{1}{2}m_1 L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2\left[L_1^2\dot{\theta}_1^2 + L_2^2(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)\right]$

Notice the structure: a quadratic form in the joint velocities with configuration-dependent coefficients — this is exactly the inertia matrix $M(\theta)$ in disguise.

6Potential energy $V$

Take $y$ upward, gravity $g$ downward, and choose $y=0$ as the reference height. Then:

$V = m_1 g y_1 + m_2 g y_2$

Substituting the heights:

$V = m_1 g L_1\sin\theta_1 + m_2 g\,[L_1\sin\theta_1 + L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)]$

Which we'll later regroup as $V = (m_1+m_2)gL_1\sin\theta_1 + m_2 g L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$.

7Lagrangian, fully expanded and collected

With $L = T - V$, expand $(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2)^2 = \dot{\theta}_1^2 + 2\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2 + \dot{\theta}_2^2$ and the cross term $2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1(\dot{\theta}_1+\dot{\theta}_2) = 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2$. After collecting like terms:

Coefficient of $\dot{\theta}_1^2$: $\;\tfrac{1}{2}m_1 L_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2}m_2[L_1^2 + L_2^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2]$

Coefficient of $\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2$: $\;m_2[L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2]$

Coefficient of $\dot{\theta}_2^2$: $\;\tfrac{1}{2}m_2 L_2^2$

Potential: $\;-(m_1+m_2)gL_1\sin\theta_1 - m_2 g L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$

This is the form we'll differentiate.

This regrouping is busywork, but it's the busywork that makes the next step painless.
Spot the inertia matrix. The three coefficients above are exactly $\tfrac{1}{2}M_{11}$, $M_{12}$, $\tfrac{1}{2}M_{22}$ for the configuration-dependent inertia matrix $M(\theta)$.

8Euler–Lagrange for $\theta_1$ → $\tau_1$

Apply $\tau_1 = \frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}_1}\right) - \frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_1}$, step by step.

(a) $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}_1} = m_1 L_1^2 \dot{\theta}_1 + m_2\!\left[(L_1^2 + L_2^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot{\theta}_1 + (L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot{\theta}_2\right]$

(b) Time derivative — and remember $\frac{d}{dt}\cos\theta_2 = -\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_2$:

$\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}_1}\right) = m_1 L_1^2 \ddot{\theta}_1 + m_2\big[(L_1^2 + L_2^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_1 + (L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_2 - 2L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2 - L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_2^2\big]$

(c) $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_1}$ from the kinetic terms is zero (no $\theta_1$ in $\cos\theta_2$), and from $V$:

$\frac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_1} = -(m_1+m_2)gL_1\cos\theta_1 - m_2 g L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$

(d) Assemble $\tau_1$:

$\boxed{\;\tau_1 = m_1 L_1^2 \ddot{\theta}_1 + m_2\!\left[(L_1^2 + L_2^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_1 + (L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_2 - 2L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2 - L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_2^2\right] + (m_1+m_2)gL_1\cos\theta_1 + m_2 g L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)\;}$

Where the Coriolis terms come from. The $-2L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2$ and $-L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_2^2$ terms aren't physics added by hand — they fall straight out of $\frac{d}{dt}\cos\theta_2$. That's the signature of Coriolis/centrifugal coupling.

9Euler–Lagrange for $\theta_2$ → $\tau_2$

Same procedure, now for the second joint.

(a) $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}_2} = m_2\!\left[(L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot{\theta}_1 + L_2^2\dot{\theta}_2\right]$

(b) Time derivative:

$\frac{d}{dt}\!\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{\theta}_2}\right) = m_2\big[(L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_1 + L_2^2\ddot{\theta}_2 - L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2\big]$

(c) $\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \theta_2}$: the kinetic part contributes a $\sin\theta_2$ term that cancels in the assembly when done carefully; the gravity contribution from $V$ gives $-m_2 g L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$.

(d) Assemble $\tau_2$:

$\boxed{\;\tau_2 = m_2\!\left[(L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\ddot{\theta}_1 + L_2^2\ddot{\theta}_2 - L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot{\theta}_1\dot{\theta}_2\right] + m_2 g L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)\;}$

10What we've built and where it goes next

We now have two coupled, second-order ODEs for $\theta_1(t)$ and $\theta_2(t)$ given the input torques $\tau_1, \tau_2$. They split cleanly into the standard manipulator form:

$\tau = M(\theta)\,\ddot{\theta} + C(\theta,\dot{\theta})\,\dot{\theta} + g(\theta)$

where $M$ is the inertia matrix (the $\ddot{\theta}$ coefficients), $C\dot{\theta}$ collects the Coriolis/centrifugal terms (the $\sin\theta_2$ velocity products), and $g(\theta)$ is the gravity vector (the $\cos$ terms). This is the form we'll plug into simulation and controller design in Module 3.
