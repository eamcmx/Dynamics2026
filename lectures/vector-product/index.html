Applications of the vector cross product

A worked problem set: from areas and volumes to torque and the Lorentz force.

Lecturer. bibliosense Course. Engineering Mathematics
This session is all problems. We've defined $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ and proved its properties — now we put it to work. We'll compute cross products by hand, read off areas and volumes from their magnitudes, build vectors perpendicular to a given pair, and then push the same machinery into physics: torque about a point and the magnetic force on a moving charge. Two sets of ten problems each. Work them in order; the second set is harder.

0 What this lecture covers

This is a problem-driven session on the vector cross product $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$. We work through two sets of ten problems each, organised by application:

  • direct computation and magnitude of $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$
  • a unit vector in the direction of the cross product
  • areas of parallelograms and triangles, volume of a parallelepiped
  • finding a vector perpendicular to two given vectors
  • the angle between two vectors via $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta$
  • torque $\boldsymbol{\tau}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$ and the moment of a force
  • the Lorentz force $\mathbf{F}_B = q\,\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}$
  • a closing problem on the projection of one cross product onto another direction

Each section below frames the relevant problems from Problems-I and Problems-II and reminds you of the formula you need.

Don't skim — do them. The cross product only sticks once your hand has cranked through a few 3×3 determinants.
Core formula. For $\mathbf{a}=(a_1,a_2,a_3)$ and $\mathbf{b}=(b_1,b_2,b_3)$, $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=\det\begin{pmatrix}\hat\imath&\hat\jmath&\hat k\\a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\end{pmatrix}$.

1 Computing a cross product and its magnitude

The mechanical starting point. Expand the symbolic determinant, collect components, and — when asked — take the Euclidean norm.

Problems-I, Q1 — compute $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ for $\mathbf{a}=3\hat\imath-4\hat\jmath+2\hat k$, $\mathbf{b}=\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath-\hat k$.

Problems-I, Q2 — for $\mathbf{u}=2\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath-4\hat k$ and $\mathbf{v}=-\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+2\hat k$, find $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|$.

Report components in the form $(x,y,z)$ and keep magnitudes exact (surds) unless a decimal is asked for.

Two sign conventions, one rule: read the determinant left-to-right, alternate $+,-,+$ on the cofactors. If you flip the order, you flip the sign of the answer.
Sign trap. $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=-(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{a})$. Get the rows in the determinant in the order the question asks.
Component formula
a × b = ( a2·b3 − a3·b2 ,
         a3·b1 − a1·b3 ,
         a1·b2 − a2·b1 )

2 Direction: a unit vector along the cross product

Once you have $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$, the unit vector in that direction is $\hat n = \dfrac{\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}}{|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|}$. By construction, $\hat n$ is perpendicular to both $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$, with orientation given by the right-hand rule.

Problems-I, Q3 — find $\hat n$ for $\mathbf{a}=4\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath+3\hat k$ and $\mathbf{b}=-\hat\imath+5\hat\jmath+4\hat k$.

Sanity check. Verify $\hat n\cdot\mathbf{a}=0$ and $\hat n\cdot\mathbf{b}=0$. If either dot product isn't zero, you've slipped a sign in the determinant.

3 Area of parallelograms and triangles

The geometric meaning of $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|$ is the area of the parallelogram spanned by $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. Halve it and you get the area of the triangle on the same two edges.

Problems-I, Q4 — area of the parallelogram on $\mathbf{p}=\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath+2\hat k$ and $\mathbf{q}=-2\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+\hat k$.

Problems-I, Q7 — area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,2,0)$, $B(3,-1,1)$, $C(0,1,4)$. Form the edge vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{AC}$, then $\text{Area}=\tfrac12|\overrightarrow{AB}\times\overrightarrow{AC}|$.

Problems-II, Q2 — area of the parallelogram on $\mathbf{a}=4\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath+\hat k$ and $\mathbf{b}=-\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath+5\hat k$.

Problems-II, Q6 — area of the triangle with vertices $A(1,0,1)$, $B(2,1,3)$, $C(0,2,1)$.

For a triangle from three points, you must build edge vectors first. The cross product of position vectors is meaningless here.
Two formulas, one idea. Parallelogram area $= |\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|$. Triangle area $= \tfrac12 |\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|$.

4 A vector perpendicular to two given vectors

Any non-zero scalar multiple of $\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ is perpendicular to both $\mathbf{a}$ and $\mathbf{b}$. So the canonical answer is $\mathbf{c}=\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ itself.

Problems-I, Q5 — find $\mathbf{c}\perp \mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}$ for $\mathbf{a}=5\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath-\hat k$ and $\mathbf{b}=2\hat\imath-\hat\jmath+4\hat k$.

Problems-II, Q4 — same task for $\mathbf{a}=5\hat\imath+4\hat\jmath+2\hat k$ and $\mathbf{b}=-\hat\imath+6\hat\jmath+3\hat k$.

Family of answers. Every $\lambda(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b})$ with $\lambda\neq 0$ works. If a unit perpendicular is asked for, normalise (see §2).

5 Volume of a parallelepiped — the scalar triple product

For three edges $\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}$ meeting at a vertex, the parallelepiped's volume is $V = \big|\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})\big| = \left|\det\begin{pmatrix}a_1&a_2&a_3\\b_1&b_2&b_3\\c_1&c_2&c_3\end{pmatrix}\right|.$

Problems-I, Q8 — volume on $\mathbf{a}=\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath+3\hat k$, $\mathbf{b}=-\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+4\hat k$, $\mathbf{c}=2\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath+\hat k$.

Take the absolute value — the determinant is signed (it tracks orientation), but a volume isn't.
Coplanarity test. If $\mathbf{a}\cdot(\mathbf{b}\times\mathbf{c})=0$, the three vectors are coplanar and the parallelepiped collapses.

6 Angle between two vectors via the cross product

From $|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|=|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|\sin\theta$ we get $\sin\theta = \dfrac{|\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}|}{|\mathbf{a}||\mathbf{b}|}$, with $0\le\theta\le\pi$.

Problems-I, Q9 — find the angle between $\mathbf{u}=3\hat\imath-\hat\jmath+2\hat k$ and $\mathbf{v}=\hat\imath+4\hat\jmath-3\hat k$ using their cross product.

The cross-product method gives you $\sin\theta$, which can't distinguish acute from obtuse on its own. If the angle could be obtuse, cross-check with $\cos\theta$ from the dot product.

7 Orthogonality check via the cross product

Two vectors $\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}$ are orthogonal iff $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$. Computing $\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}$ doesn't directly tell you that — but $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}| = |\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|$ does, since $\sin\theta=1$ iff $\theta=\pi/2$.

Problems-II, Q7 — for $\mathbf{u}=2\hat\imath+5\hat\jmath-3\hat k$ and $\mathbf{v}=4\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+\hat k$, compute the cross product, take its magnitude, and compare with $|\mathbf{u}||\mathbf{v}|$.

In practice the dot product is the cleaner orthogonality test. The question wants you to see the relationship between $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|$ and $\sin\theta$.
Watch the units. $|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|^2 + (\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v})^2 = |\mathbf{u}|^2|\mathbf{v}|^2$ — Lagrange's identity. A useful cross-check.

8 Torque and the moment of a force

For a force $\mathbf{F}$ applied at position $\mathbf{r}$ relative to a reference point, the torque (or moment of force) about that point is $\boldsymbol\tau = \mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}.$ The magnitude $|\boldsymbol\tau|$ has units of $\text{N·m}$; its direction follows the right-hand rule (axis of rotation).

Problems-I, Q6 — $\mathbf{F}=7\hat\imath-3\hat\jmath+2\hat k\,\text{N}$ at $\mathbf{r}=3\hat\imath+\hat\jmath-4\hat k\,\text{m}$, torque about the origin.

Problems-II, Q1 — $\mathbf{F}=3\hat\imath+4\hat\jmath-2\hat k\,\text{N}$ at $\mathbf{r}=2\hat\imath-\hat\jmath+3\hat k\,\text{m}$, torque about the origin.

Problems-II, Q3 — $\mathbf{F}=7\hat\imath-2\hat\jmath+4\hat k\,\text{N}$ at $\mathbf{r}=4\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath-3\hat k\,\text{m}$, moment of force about the point.

Problems-II, Q9 — wrench on a bolt: $\mathbf{F}=8\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath-5\hat k\,\text{N}$, moment arm $\mathbf{r}=-3\hat\imath+4\hat\jmath+\hat k\,\text{m}$. Compute $\boldsymbol\tau$.

$\mathbf{r}$ is the position vector of the point where the force is applied, measured from the pivot. Get that vector wrong and the rest is theatre.
Order matters. $\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{F}$, not $\mathbf{F}\times\mathbf{r}$. Swapping flips the sign and reverses the rotation axis.

9 Aside: work done by a force

Strictly, this one is a dot-product problem — included in Problems-II as a contrast to the cross-product applications around it.

Work done by a constant force $\mathbf{F}$ over a straight displacement $\mathbf{s}$ is $W = \mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{s}$.

Problems-II, Q5 — $\mathbf{F}=6\hat\imath+3\hat\jmath-2\hat k\,\text{N}$, motion in the direction $\mathbf{d}=2\hat\imath-\hat\jmath+4\hat k$ over a distance of $5\,\text{m}$. Build $\mathbf{s}=5\,\hat d$ where $\hat d=\mathbf{d}/|\mathbf{d}|$, then evaluate $|W| = |\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{s}|$.

If you reach for the cross product here, stop. Work projects force onto displacement — that's the dot product's job.
Why this lives in a cross-product set. To force you to choose the right product. Recognising which physical quantity needs which product is half the skill.

10 Magnetic force on a moving charge — the Lorentz force

A charge $q$ moving with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ through a magnetic field $\mathbf{B}$ experiences $\mathbf{F}_B = q\,\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}.$ The force is perpendicular to the plane of $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{B}$, so the magnetic field does no work — it only bends the trajectory.

Problems-I, Q10 — $q=1.5\,\mu\text{C}$, $\mathbf{v}=2\hat\imath-3\hat\jmath+\hat k\,\text{m/s}$, $\mathbf{B}=5\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath-4\hat k\,\text{T}$.

Problems-II, Q8 — $q=2\,\mu\text{C}$, $\mathbf{v}=3\hat\imath+2\hat\jmath-\hat k\,\text{m/s}$, $\mathbf{B}=4\hat\imath-\hat\jmath+3\hat k\,\text{T}$.

Keep careful track of the $10^{-6}$ from $\mu\text{C}$ and report $\mathbf{F}_B$ in newtons.

Sign of $q$ matters: a negative charge reverses the direction of $\mathbf{F}_B$. Both problems here use positive $q$.
Units. $[\mathbf{F}_B] = \text{C}\cdot(\text{m/s})\cdot\text{T} = \text{N}$. Always check by walking the units through.

11 Projection of a cross product onto a direction

A neat closer that combines two operations. The scalar projection of a vector $\mathbf{w}$ onto a direction $\mathbf{u}$ is $\dfrac{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}}{|\mathbf{u}|}$.

Problems-II, Q10 — for $\mathbf{p}=6\hat\imath-3\hat\jmath+2\hat k$ and $\mathbf{q}=\hat\imath+4\hat\jmath-2\hat k$, compute $\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q}$, then project the result onto $\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+\hat k$: $\text{proj} = \dfrac{(\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q})\cdot(\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+\hat k)}{|\hat\imath+\hat\jmath+\hat k|}.$

Notice the underlying object: $(\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{q})\cdot\mathbf{r}$ is a scalar triple product — geometrically, the signed volume of the parallelepiped on $\mathbf{p},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{r}$.
Vector vs scalar projection. Scalar: $\dfrac{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}}{|\mathbf{u}|}$. Vector: $\dfrac{\mathbf{w}\cdot\mathbf{u}}{|\mathbf{u}|^2}\mathbf{u}$. Read the question carefully.
