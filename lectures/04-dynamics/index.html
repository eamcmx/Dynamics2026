Lagrangian dynamics of a 2-link planar arm

From transformation matrices to joint torques — every step, no algebra skipped.

Lecturer. bibliosense Module. Module 2 — Dynamics
Today we walk the full Lagrangian pipeline on the canonical 2-link planar arm with tip masses m₁ and m₂. We start where every robotics problem starts — the homogeneous transforms — and end with the explicit torque equations τ₁ and τ₂ that Module 3 will plug into the Euler–Lagrange machinery. No leaps, no hand-waving: every square is expanded, every cross-term collected, every chain-rule term written out. By the end you should be able to reproduce this on a blank page.

0 What this lecture covers

This is a fully worked solution for the 2-link planar robot arm with point masses m₁ and m₂ at the distal end of each link. We will derive, in order: the homogeneous transformation matrices, the Cartesian positions of the tip masses, their velocities, the squared speeds v₁² and v₂², the kinetic energy T, the potential energy V, the Lagrangian L = T − V, and finally the joint torques τ₁ and τ₂ via the Euler–Lagrange equations.

The goal is pedagogical: show every algebraic step so that the reader sees exactly where each term in the manipulator equation comes from.

Skipping steps is how dynamics derivations go wrong — so we don't skip any.
Setup. Planar 2-link arm, joint angles θ₁ and θ₂, link lengths L₁ and L₂, point masses m₁, m₂ at the end of each link. Gravity acts in −y.

1 Homogeneous transformation matrices

We attach a frame to each link and write the homogeneous transformation from the base. T₁ takes us to the end of link 1 (where m₁ sits); T₂ takes us to the end of link 2 (where m₂ sits), expressed directly in the base frame by composing the two rotations.

Notice that the rotation in T₂ is θ₁ + θ₂ — that is the key fact that makes every subsequent derivative trigonometrically clean.

T₁ — base to end of link 1
T1 = | cos θ1   -sin θ1   L1 cos θ1 |
     | sin θ1    cos θ1   L1 sin θ1 |
     |   0         0           1     |
T₂ — base to end of link 2
T2 = | cos(θ1+θ2)  -sin(θ1+θ2)   L1 cos θ1 + L2 cos(θ1+θ2) |
     | sin(θ1+θ2)   cos(θ1+θ2)   L1 sin θ1 + L2 sin(θ1+θ2) |
     |    0             0                       1            |

2 Cartesian positions of the tip masses

Read the translation column off each transformation. For m₁:

$x_1 = L_1\cos\theta_1, \quad y_1 = L_1\sin\theta_1$

For m₂:

$x_2 = L_1\cos\theta_1 + L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$

$y_2 = L_1\sin\theta_1 + L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$

These are the only geometric facts we need — everything that follows is calculus on these four expressions.

3 Velocities by the chain rule

Differentiate each position with respect to time, remembering that θ₁ and θ₂ are both functions of t.

For m₁:

$\dot x_1 = -L_1\sin\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1, \quad \dot y_1 = L_1\cos\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1$

For m₂:

$\dot x_2 = -L_1\sin\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1 - L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)$

$\dot y_2 = L_1\cos\theta_1\,\dot\theta_1 + L_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)$

The factor $(\dot\theta_1 + \dot\theta_2)$ appears because the angle of the second link in the base frame is $\theta_1 + \theta_2$.

Watch the chain rule. $\frac{d}{dt}\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) = -\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)$ — both rates contribute.

4 Squared speeds v₁² and v₂²

For m₁ the algebra collapses immediately via $\sin^2 + \cos^2 = 1$:

$v_1^2 = \dot x_1^2 + \dot y_1^2 = L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2$

For m₂ we expand the squares fully and group:

$v_2^2 = L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2 + L_2^2(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_1(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)$

The cross-term simplifies because $\sin\theta_1\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2) + \cos\theta_1\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2) = \cos\theta_2$ — the angle-difference identity is what produces the famous $\cos\theta_2$ coupling that you see in every 2-link manipulator equation.

That $\cos\theta_2$ is the whole reason this system is nonlinear — remember it.
Identity used. $\sin\alpha\sin\beta + \cos\alpha\cos\beta = \cos(\alpha - \beta)$. Here $\alpha = \theta_1+\theta_2$, $\beta = \theta_1$, so the difference is $\theta_2$.

Derive v₂² · pick which identity to apply next

practice

Start from the fully expanded six-term square and apply identities one at a time: sin²+cos²=1 for each angle, then the angle-difference identity for the cross-terms. The cosθ₂ coupling falls out at the end.

5 Kinetic energy T

Sum the two point-mass kinetic energies:

$T = \tfrac{1}{2} m_1 v_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} m_2 v_2^2$

Substitute the explicit expressions:

$T = \tfrac{1}{2} m_1 L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2 + \tfrac{1}{2} m_2\left[L_1^2\dot\theta_1^2 + L_2^2(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_1(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)\right]$

6 Potential energy V

Take y upward and gravity g downward. Each mass contributes mgy:

$V = m_1 g y_1 + m_2 g y_2$

$V = m_1 g L_1 \sin\theta_1 + m_2 g\left[L_1\sin\theta_1 + L_2\sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)\right]$

Grouping by mass-times-arm gives the clean form $(m_1 + m_2) g L_1 \sin\theta_1 + m_2 g L_2 \sin(\theta_1+\theta_2)$ that we will use when differentiating.

7 The Lagrangian and its expansion

With $L = T - V$ in hand, expand $(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2)^2 = \dot\theta_1^2 + 2\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2 + \dot\theta_2^2$ and the cross-term $2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_1(\dot\theta_1+\dot\theta_2) = 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_1^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2\,\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2$.

Collect like terms by velocity product. The Lagrangian becomes a sum of $\dot\theta_1^2$, $\dot\theta_1\dot\theta_2$, and $\dot\theta_2^2$ pieces (the kinetic part) minus the gravity potential.

Why we expand. The Euler–Lagrange operator wants partials in $\dot\theta_i$ and $\theta_i$ separately. Pre-expanding the squares means each partial reads off by inspection.
Collected coefficients of velocity products
θ̇1² :  ½ m1 L1² + ½ m2 (L1² + L2² + 2 L1 L2 cos θ2)
θ̇1·θ̇2 :  m2 (L2² + L1 L2 cos θ2)
θ̇2² :  ½ m2 L2²

V :  (m1 + m2) g L1 sin θ1 + m2 g L2 sin(θ1+θ2)

8 Euler–Lagrange for θ₁ → τ₁

Compute $\partial L/\partial \dot\theta_1$, take its time derivative (remembering $\cos\theta_2$ is time-varying so it contributes $-\sin\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2$ terms), then subtract $\partial L/\partial \theta_1$.

$\dfrac{\partial L}{\partial \dot\theta_1} = m_1 L_1^2 \dot\theta_1 + m_2\left[(L_1^2 + L_2^2 + 2L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot\theta_1 + (L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot\theta_2\right]$

The potential contributes $-(m_1+m_2)gL_1\cos\theta_1 - m_2gL_2\cos(\theta_1+\theta_2)$ to $\partial L/\partial\theta_1$ (the kinetic term has no $\theta_1$ dependence — only $\theta_2$ appears via $\cos\theta_2$).

Final τ₁
τ1 =  m1 L1² θ̈1
    + m2 [ (L1² + L2² + 2 L1 L2 cos θ2) θ̈1
         + (L2² + L1 L2 cos θ2) θ̈2
         - 2 L1 L2 sin θ2 · θ̇2 θ̇1
         -   L1 L2 sin θ2 · θ̇2² ]
    + (m1 + m2) g L1 cos θ1
    + m2 g L2 cos(θ1 + θ2)

9 Euler–Lagrange for θ₂ → τ₂

Now $\partial L/\partial \dot\theta_2 = m_2\left[(L_2^2 + L_1L_2\cos\theta_2)\dot\theta_1 + L_2^2 \dot\theta_2\right]$.

Its time derivative carries one $-L_1L_2\sin\theta_2\,\dot\theta_2\dot\theta_1$ term from differentiating $\cos\theta_2$. The partial $\partial L/\partial\theta_2$ picks up both a kinetic piece (from the $\cos\theta_2$ couplings) and a gravity piece $-m_2 g L_2 \cos(\theta_1 + \theta_2)$.

Final τ₂
τ2 =  m2 [ (L2² + L1 L2 cos θ2) θ̈1
         +  L2² θ̈2
         -  L1 L2 sin θ2 · θ̇2 θ̇1 ]
    + m2 g L2 cos(θ1 + θ2)

practice

Don't copy τ₁ and edit. Build τ₂ from the partials and notice the famous trap — the −L₁L₂sinθ₂·θ̇₁² term lives in τ₁ but is absent from τ₂.

10 What we have, and where it leads

From two homogeneous transforms we now have closed-form torque equations τ₁ and τ₂ in terms of the joint state $(\theta_1, \theta_2, \dot\theta_1, \dot\theta_2, \ddot\theta_1, \ddot\theta_2)$. These can be rearranged into the standard manipulator form $M(\theta)\ddot\theta + C(\theta,\dot\theta)\dot\theta + g(\theta) = \tau$, which is what Module 3 builds the controllers on.
